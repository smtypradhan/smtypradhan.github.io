Schuster Vendor Payment Intelligence

Two-stage ML pipeline: K-Means payer segmentation + Random Forest late-payment prediction — 81% precision across 94K invoices and $466M in open receivables.

View code

The problem

Two-thirds (66%) of Schuster's 94K historical invoices were paid late, straining cash flow and supplier relationships. The collections team treated every account the same, and the top 10 customers held 94% of all receivables, so a few slow payers carried most of the risk.

Stakeholders

  • Collections teamPrimary users of the risk-ranked open-invoice list and the segment playbooks for follow-up.
  • Finance / accounts-receivable leadershipAudience for the 20-slide findings deck on cash-flow risk, concentration and payment-term policy.
  • Key-account owners for the top 10 customersGiven per-customer late-payment profiles (e.g., ~175 vs ~35 days late on average) to tailor treatment.

Approach

  1. Cleaned 94K received-payment records: removed invalid amounts, trimmed outliers at the 1st–99th percentile, and engineered the late-payment target plus payment-term and payment-time features.
  2. EDA surfaced where lateness concentrates: SAR, USD and AED invoices; goods over services; 30/60-day terms from invoice date; and repeating weekly spikes.
  3. Segmented customers with K-Means on mean and variability of payment time, choosing k = 3 by silhouette score: Punctual, Erratic and Variable payers.
  4. Fed the segment label into a Random Forest classifier, tuned with 5-fold GridSearchCV and optimized for the late-payment class.
  5. Scored every open invoice through the same pipeline to produce a prioritized, risk-ranked collections list.

Solution

A reproducible notebook pipeline (EDA → segmentation → prediction → open-invoice scoring) plus a stakeholder deck that turns model output into segment-specific collection playbooks.

Impact

81% Late-payment precision when the model flags an invoice as late, it is right 4 times in 5
$466M Open receivables scored 78K open invoices risk-ranked for the collections team
94K Invoices modeled historical payments across 690 customer accounts
94% Receivables concentration held by the top 10 customers — where collections effort pays off most

Business outcome

  • Moved collections from contacting every account the same way to a ranked worklist: on a 66% late base rate, the model's flags are correct 81% of the time.
  • Gave each payer segment its own playbook: early-payment discounts and rewards for Punctual payers, explicit term agreements for Erratic payers, and flexible schedules with personal follow-up for Variable payers.
  • Recommended moving 30/60-day invoice-date terms to end-of-month terms (15 or 60 days EOM), which showed better on-time compliance.
  • Gave the team a seasonal calendar to add collections capacity before the late-payment spikes in late January, mid-April and late May.

What I'd carry forward

Optimizing for the class the business acts on mattered more than overall accuracy (64%). A model that is trustworthy when it says 'late' is what lets a collections team act on it.
