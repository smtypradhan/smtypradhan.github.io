X Education Lead Scoring Model

Logistic-regression lead scoring (0–100) to lift an online-education sales team off a ~30% conversion rate: AUC 0.97, and 92% of flagged 'hot' leads converted.

View code

The problem

X Education generated plenty of leads but converted only about 30% of them. The CEO's target was 80%. Without any way to rank leads, the sales team spent equal time on every lead, most of whom would never buy.

Stakeholders

  • CEOSet the 80% conversion target and was the audience for the executive summary and recommendations.
  • Sales teamPrimary users of the 0–100 lead scores, using them to decide who to call first and who to move to email nurturing.
  • Seasonal sales interns (~10 per cycle)Given an 'aggressive mode' call list for the two-month intern period, when the goal is to reach as many likely converters as possible.
  • MarketingGiven recommendations on website engagement, SMS outreach and targeting management specializations.

Approach

  1. Cleaned ~9,000 leads across 37 fields: dropped columns over 40% empty or filled in by sales only after a call, added an 'Others' category instead of losing the 37% of leads with no specialization, and standardized inconsistent labels.
  2. EDA found that time spent on the website was the strongest behavioral signal (r = 0.36 with conversion) and that visits and page views overlapped heavily (r = 0.77).
  3. Dummy-encoded the data to 81 features, used RFE to narrow it to 20, then refined over 9 GLM iterations, dropping variables with high p-values and VIF scores above 5.
  4. Settled on a 12-variable logistic regression, chose a 0.38 probability cutoff from the sensitivity/specificity trade-off, and turned probabilities into 0–100 lead scores.
  5. Turned the model's coefficients into two sales playbooks: one for intern season and one for quarters when the target is already met.

Solution

A lead scoring pipeline that gives every lead a 0–100 conversion score, plus a stakeholder deck, an executive summary and scenario playbooks that tell the sales team who to call, who to email and who to skip.

Impact

0.97 AUC-ROC near-perfect separation of converters from non-converters
91.8% Hot-lead precision of leads the model flags as likely to convert, about 92% did (training set)
91.9% Held-out accuracy with 96% sensitivity on the test set, so the model generalizes rather than overfits
30% → 80% Baseline → target the conversion gap the scoring model was built to close

Business outcome

  • Showed a path to the CEO's 80% target: leads the model flags as likely to convert did convert about 92% of the time historically, compared with a ~30% baseline across all leads.
  • Gave sales a 'conservation mode' for quarters when the target is met early: call only leads scoring 80 or more, move mid-tier leads to automated email, and drop leads tagged 'Ringing', 'Might be' or 'Worst' to cut wasted calls.
  • Gave the intern season an 'aggressive mode' call list prioritizing working professionals, highly engaged website visitors, SMS-responsive leads and management specializations, plus time-limited sign-up incentives.
  • Pointed marketing to the strongest signals of intent: website engagement, form fills (Lead Add Form) and SMS activity.

What I'd carry forward

Keeping the model interpretable was the point. A 12-variable logistic regression with readable coefficients gave the sales team rules they could act on, like 'call working professionals who got an SMS', not just a score they had to take on faith.
